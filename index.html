About

From the time I can remember, I've had a burning need to understand where the world came from, how it works, and where it is heading. Growing up, that meant absorbing everything — newspapers, television, books, the internet, social media, podcasts and so on. Newspapers gave us a daily record of the world. Television let us watch it as it happened. Podcasts let anyone go deep on anything. Social media handed the audience a microphone. Each layer moved the public closer to the information. But none of them let people price what they believe.

Prediction markets are where that gap closes for me. Take a question like "Will there be an Ebola pandemic in 2026?" A headline about an outbreak is written to be read. A viral post is written to be shared. Neither pays a price for being wrong. But when someone places a bet on that question, they have to weigh everything they've read against what they're willing to lose — and the market price that emerges is a signal none of the other layers can produce: what people actually believe, with money behind it. That's not a replacement for existing media — it's a new layer on top of it, one that lets society collectively build a more accurate picture of the world.

The question I'm sitting with now: understanding where existing prediction markets like Polymarket and Kalshi fall short, and whether it's a gap I can solve.

Passion for Crypto

What struck me most is that these aren't just financial networks. They're self-governing systems — networks that organize resources, coordinate strangers, and evolve their own rules without central control. The idea that you can align stakeholders and users by design, rather than by institution, is the most interesting organizational primitive I've encountered. Most people see prices. I see a new way of organizing human effort, still in its earliest chapters.

Skill
